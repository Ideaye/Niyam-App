What this is — and is not. These are AI reviewers with native-level fluency, reviewing adversarially (they did not write this content). Uncertainties are marked in-line where reviewers flagged them (two Unicode rendering questions need a real-device check).

2. Cross-language systemic findings (fix as classes, not per-language patches)

  • S1 — 9 pause-behaviour strings missing from ALL 7 locales. onb_pause_title, settings_row_pause, settings_pause_title, pause_length_label, pause_secs_fmt, pause_interval_toggle, pause_interval_caption, pause_freq_label, pause_mins_fmt — the v1.0.4 pause feature's strings were never swept into any locale. Every non-English user hits an all-English onboarding step 5 and Settings section.
  • S2 — the permission screens were translated from a superseded English source. The 2026-06-16 T3 rewrite (plainer copy, "why" explanations, genericized app names) never reached the locales. Consequences repeated in every language reviewed: perm_usage_title inverts the actor (tells the user to detect app opens, instead of asking permission for Niyam); perm_usage_body drops the privacy assurance "It only sees which app is in front — never what you do inside it" (a Play-policy under-disclosure, not just a fidelity gap); several bodies still hardcode "Instagram, Facebook, YouTube" where English says "your blocked apps"; bodies are verbless purpose fragments. Class fix: retranslate the whole perm_* family in all 7 locales from the current English.
  • S3 — Gita 3.35 meaning inverted in Bengali, Kannada, Gujarati (and improvable in Tamil/Hindi): the pronoun "that path" binds to your own path in those languages' word order, reversing para-dharmo bhayāvahaḥ. Fix by naming the referent ("the other's path") explicitly in all languages.
  • S4 — perm_battery_title: "don't close us in the background" — the only first-person self-reference in the app (it is नियम/నియమ్/… everywhere else), and it blames the user for what the OS does. Same fix shape in all 7: "Let Niyam stay awake."
  • S5 — app-voice register break in exactly two meanings everywhere: om-namah-shivaya and hare-krishna address the user with informal singular (तुम/నువ్వు/ನೀನು/তুমি/நீ) while the whole UI is respectful-plural. (Scripture speaking to Arjuna in the Gita entries is correct and stays.) Fix both entries in all 7 languages.
  • S6 — in-script names/scripture labels follow uniform Sanskrit transliteration, not each language's print convention, and contradict the app's own filter chips on the same screen (pack "భగవద్గీతా" vs chip "భగవద్గీత"; pack "ஸ்தோத்ர" vs chip "ஸ்தோத்திரங்கள்"; pack "मन्त्र" vs Marathi print "मंत्र"…). Root cause is tools/generate_names.py emitting one convention for all. Fix: per-language display-form pass over names + sourceLabels (each language section lists exact changes).
  • S7 — "Signed in as" label: all 7 reviewers independently said change it to "Signed-in account" shape — the current forms are dangling sentences above an email value.
  • S8 — deity_universal: all 7 said the current word reads as "general/widespread/public", not "not tied to one deity". Per-language proposals in the sections below.
  • S9 — Focus intention: change to the ekāgratā word in Hindi, Telugu, Bengali, Gujarati (ध्यान/ధ్యానం reads as meditation); Marathi and Kannada already have it right — keep.
  • S10 — Unicode rendering risks to verify on a real device: Tamil visarga U+A789 (Latin-block codepoint → font-fallback/tofu risk; Tamil reviewer recommends ஃ), Telugu candrabindu U+0C00 ×4 in the Chalisa (reviewer recommends anusvara ం), Kannada candrabindu U+0C81 (verify only). Gujarati's candrabindu/anusvara split was checked mark-by-mark and adjudicated correct (matches Gita Press) — that old flag is a false positive.
  • S11 — Bengali numerals: go all-Bengali digits (hardcoded Latin "7", "30", "60" sit next to locale-formatted ৪/১৪; Android formats %d as ০-৯ under bn). Device-verify before bulk edit.
  • S12 — postpositions/case suffixes glued to placeholders or detached by spaces (Marathi "%2$s चे", Kannada "%2$s ನ %1$d ದಿನಗಳು" — broken genitive on the celebration screen, Gujarati "%1$s માં", Tamil "%1$d இல்"): restructure those format strings to avoid inflecting a placeholder.

3. Errors in the ENGLISH reference itself (meaning.en)

  • ram-raksha-opening: EN says "a hundred million ślokas"; śatakoṭi = 100 crore = a billion. Hindi, Kannada and Telugu translations are all correct — fix the English, keep them.
  • hanuman-chalisa-opening: EN glosses "lord of the three worlds"; the chaupai it sits beside says tihu loka ujāgara — "illuminator of the three worlds". Every Hindi reader knows this line by heart. Fix EN + the translations that followed it.
